The Swing of Golf Club
======================

The dynamics of golf swing is very complex and varied with different golfers and clubs they
use. Many swing parameters would affect the impact speed and direction of a clubhead on golf
ball. The application of Newton's laws of motion to a simple swing model may bring some
understanding of its mechanism, and we can thus search for the best way to improve the swing
performance accordingly.

In this program we apply a two-rod model (or called double pendulum model) to simulate the golf swing.
Since the Lagrangian function of two-rod model is simple
and one can easily extract arm and wrist-cock torques, which are the major parameters in swing
system. The torque from shoulder joint are combined with the torque generated by golfer's
trunk as an "arm torque". In two-rod model we do not need to consider the shoulder mass or
moment of inertia in the swing, which are not easy to measure or define and do not directly
affect the clubhead velocity. On the other hand, we take into account the bend of arm for the
full-length backswing.

Two-Rod Model
-------------

The Lagrangian function of two-rod model is described in [Landau1976]_ and firstly applied by Theodore P.
Jorgensen [Jorgensen1999]_ on the golf swing. This model can be also applied in the computer game of golf [Bourg2015]_.

We define :math:`\theta` as the angle through the arm has rotated clockwise from the downward direction.
:math:`\alpha` represents the angle through the arm that is rotated counter-clockwise in the downswing direction.
:math:`\beta` represents the wrist-cock angle measured clockwise from the extension of the arm.
The acceleration vector generated by a golfer is :math:`\vec{a} = a_x \hat{x} - a_y \hat{y}`.

The potential energy of the two-rod system measured from the arm axis is:

.. math::

   PE = -(S_A + M_C R)[(g \sin\varphi - a_y)\cos\theta + a_x \sin\theta]
        - S_C[(g \sin\varphi - a_y)\cos(\theta + \beta) + a_x \sin(\theta + \beta)]

The clubhead velocity is calculated as:

.. math::

   V_C = \sqrt{(R^2 + L^2 + 2RL\cos\beta)\dot{\alpha}^2 + L^2\dot{\beta}^2 - 2(L^2 + RL\cos\beta)\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta}}

where :math:`\dot{\alpha} = d\alpha/dt` and :math:`\dot{\beta} = d\beta/dt` are angular velocities.

The angle of clubhead rotated clockwise from the downward direction is:

.. math::

   \theta_C = \theta + \beta = (\theta_0 - \alpha) + \beta

Note that the variable :math:`\theta = \theta_0 - \alpha`, so :math:`\dot{\theta} = -\dot{\alpha}`.

The kinetic energy of the two-rod system is:

.. math::

   KE = \frac{1}{2}(J + I + M_C R^2 + 2RS_C\cos\beta)\dot{\alpha}^2
        + \frac{1}{2}I\dot{\beta}^2
        - (I + RS_C\cos\beta)\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta}

By the Lagrangian differential equations, the explicit forms of :math:`Q_\alpha` and :math:`Q_\beta` are:

.. math::

   Q_\alpha &= (J + I + M_C R^2 + 2RS_C\cos\beta)\ddot{\alpha}
            - (I + RS_C\cos\beta)\ddot{\beta}
            + (\dot{\beta}^2 - 2\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta})RS_C\sin\beta \\
            &\quad - S_C[(g\sin\varphi - a_y)\sin(\theta+\beta) - a_x\cos(\theta+\beta)] \\
            &\quad - (S_A + M_C R)[(g\sin\varphi - a_y)\sin\theta - a_x\cos\theta]

.. math::

   Q_\beta &= I\ddot{\beta}
           - (I + RS_C\cos\beta)\ddot{\alpha}
           + \dot{\alpha}^2 RS_C\sin\beta \\
           &\quad + S_C[(g\sin\varphi - a_y)\sin(\theta+\beta) - a_x\cos(\theta+\beta)]

Rearranging to obtain explicit forms of :math:`\ddot{\alpha}` and :math:`\ddot{\beta}`:

.. math::

   \ddot{\alpha} = f_{\ddot{\alpha}}(\dot{\alpha}, \beta, \dot{\beta}, \ddot{\beta}, \theta)

.. math::

   \ddot{\beta} = f_{\ddot{\beta}}(\dot{\alpha}, \ddot{\alpha}, \beta, \theta)

First and Second Moments of Arm
--------------------------------

By definition, the first and second moments of arm, :math:`S_A` and :math:`J`, are calculated as:

.. math::

   S_A = \sum_i m_i l_i, \qquad J = \sum_i m_i l_i^2

where :math:`m_i` and :math:`l_i` are the small elements of mass and rotational radius of arm from the arm axis.
There are two types of backswing: restricted backswing (Type I) and full-length backswing (Type II).

Type I: Restricted Backswing
^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^

For Type I, the arm is not bent during the swing. The second moment of arm is:

.. math::

   J = 2\rho_{A1}\left[R_S^2 r_A + \frac{r_A^3}{3} - R_S r_A^2\cos\delta\right]_0^{R_A/2}
     + 2\rho_{A2}\left[R_S^2 r_A + \frac{r_A^3}{3} - R_S r_A^2\cos\delta\right]_{R_A/2}^{R_A}

where :math:`R_S` and :math:`R_A` are lengths of shoulder and arm. The densities of upper arm and forearm are
:math:`\rho_{A1} = M_{A1}/R_{A1}` and :math:`\rho_{A2} = M_{A2}/R_{A2}`.

The angle :math:`\delta` and effective length of arm :math:`R` are:

.. math::

   \delta = \cos^{-1}\frac{R_S}{R_A}, \qquad R = \sqrt{R_A^2 - R_S^2}

This integration is solved by Maxima [Maxima]_.
The :math:`S_A`, :math:`J` and :math:`R` for Type I backswing are **constants** during the swing.

Type II: Full-Length Backswing
^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^

For Type II, the right arm is bent at the start of swing and gradually straightens before impact.
The :math:`S_A`, :math:`J` and :math:`R` are **varied with time** during the swing, depending on the bending angle :math:`\omega`.
The percentages of weights for male, female and average are from [BodySegment]_.

First and Second Moments of Club
---------------------------------

The first and second moments of club, :math:`S_C` and :math:`I`, are calculated as:

.. math::

   S_C = \sum_j m_j l_j, \qquad I = \sum_j m_j l_j^2

The mass center of a club from wrist-cock axis :math:`L` is:

.. math::

   L = \frac{m_\text{shaft} \times L_\text{shaft}/2 + m_\text{head} \times (L_\text{shaft} + L_\text{head}/2)}{M_C}

where :math:`M_C = m_\text{shaft} + m_\text{head}`. The moments are:

.. math::

   S_C = M_C \times L, \qquad I = M_C \times L^2

Numerical Solutions of Two-Rod Model
-------------------------------------

Since the equations for :math:`\ddot{\alpha}` and :math:`\ddot{\beta}` are non-linear and coupled, there
is no analytical solution. It is a typical chaos system and we can only use
numerical methods to approach the solution. Four Runge-Kutta-based methods are provided. Solution 4 (the
default) is the classical fourth-order Runge-Kutta method; Solutions 1--3 are the original methods of this
model and are kept for comparison.

Solution 1
^^^^^^^^^^

Given the initial conditions of :math:`\alpha, \dot{\alpha}, \ddot{\alpha}, \beta, \dot{\beta}, \ddot{\beta}` and :math:`\theta`,
we compute the new :math:`\ddot{\alpha}` and :math:`\ddot{\beta}` independently. We integrate with fourth-order Runge-Kutta
to obtain :math:`\dot{\alpha}` and :math:`\dot{\beta}`, then integrate with Euler method to obtain :math:`\alpha` and :math:`\beta`:

.. math::

   \alpha_{n+1} = \alpha_n + \dot{\alpha}_{n+1} h, \qquad
   \beta_{n+1} = \beta_n + \dot{\beta}_{n+1} h

where :math:`h` is a small time interval (h = 0.0001 sec). This method is reasonable while the correlation
between :math:`\ddot{\alpha}` and :math:`\ddot{\beta}` is small.

Solution 2
^^^^^^^^^^

Given initial conditions, we compute :math:`\ddot{\alpha}` first and then substitute it into the equation
for :math:`\ddot{\beta}`. This method takes the correlation between :math:`\ddot{\alpha}` and :math:`\ddot{\beta}` into account,
considering the average effects of next four and three steps.

Solution 3
^^^^^^^^^^

Given initial conditions, we compute :math:`\ddot{\beta}` first and then substitute it into the equation
for :math:`\ddot{\alpha}`. This method also accounts for the correlation, considering the average effects of
next three and four steps of :math:`\ddot{\alpha}` and :math:`\ddot{\beta}`.

In Solutions 1--3 the angles :math:`\alpha, \beta`, the arm geometry (:math:`R, J, S_A`) and the torques are held
fixed within each time step, and :math:`\alpha, \beta` are advanced with the Euler step above. The overall accuracy
is therefore first order in :math:`h`: halving :math:`h` halves the error. With :math:`h = 0.0001` sec, the clubhead
impact velocity is within about 0.06 m/s, and the impact angle within about 0.2 degree, of the converged solution.

Solution 4
^^^^^^^^^^

The coupled equations for :math:`\ddot{\alpha}` and :math:`\ddot{\beta}` are linear in the two accelerations, so at
any state they can be solved exactly as a :math:`2 \times 2` linear system:

.. math::

   \begin{pmatrix} A & -B \\ -B & I \end{pmatrix}
   \begin{pmatrix} \ddot{\alpha} \\ \ddot{\beta} \end{pmatrix} =
   \begin{pmatrix} r_\alpha \\ r_\beta \end{pmatrix}, \qquad
   A = J + I + M_C R^2 + 2 R S_C \cos\beta, \quad B = I + R S_C \cos\beta

where :math:`r_\alpha` and :math:`r_\beta` collect the torque, velocity and gravity terms. The state
:math:`(\alpha, \dot{\alpha}, \beta, \dot{\beta})` is then integrated with the classical fourth-order Runge-Kutta method,
re-evaluating the arm geometry, :math:`\theta` and the torques at every stage. The start time of the wrist-cock
torque is resolved within the time step. The error is fourth order in :math:`h`, so a larger step
:math:`h = 0.0004` sec is used: the impact velocity and angle are then within 0.001 m/s and 0.003 degree of the
converged solution, and the simulation runs about twice as fast as Solutions 1--3.

For every method, the last time step is interpolated so that the reported impact values are at exactly the
impact arm angle.

Case 2: The Effect of Arm Torque
^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^

Varying the arm torque :math:`Q_\alpha` and re-optimizing wrist-cock torque :math:`Q_\beta`:
the larger the arm torque, the larger the optimized wrist-cock torque in the opposite direction is needed,
and the greater the clubhead velocity at impact.

Case 3: The Effect of Wrist-Cock Torque
^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^

Varying the starting angle :math:`t_{\theta}` from 135 to 120 degrees:
the larger the time delay of wrist-cock torque, the greater the clubhead impact velocity.
However, it encounters a critical point where :math:`d\beta/dQ_\beta \approx 0`, resulting in large systematic errors.

Case 4: The Effect of Initial Angles
^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^

Varying initial arm and wrist-cock angles :math:`\theta_0` and :math:`\beta_0`:
the larger :math:`\theta_0` and :math:`\beta_0`, the greater the clubhead impact velocity.

Case 5: Type I vs Type II Backswings
^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^

For Type II (full-length backswing), the arm bending angle :math:`\omega` evolves with time as:

.. math::

   \omega(t) = \text{Min}(\omega) + \frac{\alpha(t)}{\theta_0}[\text{Max}(\omega) - \text{Min}(\omega)]

In Type II swing, the effective arm length :math:`R`, and the moments :math:`S_A` and :math:`J` vary with time.

Case 6: The Effect of Clubhead Mass
^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^

The launched velocity of a golf ball [Wesson2009]_ is:

.. math::

   V_\text{ball} = (1 + \text{COR}) \times \frac{M}{M + m} \times V_C

where :math:`M` and :math:`m` are the mass of clubhead and golf ball, and COR is the coefficient of restitution.
